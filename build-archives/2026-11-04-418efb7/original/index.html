Explore connections

Related. Not equivalent.

These editorial comparisons connect questions across the work. They are interpretations to inspect, rather than universal causal rules.

  • Delivered ≠ Complete

    A delivery status can precede verification of document sufficiency. Completion depends on the relevant acceptance check.

    Why distinguish them?

    A sending status is convenient to count. Verification asks whether the receiving role has what it needs; moving the check and sharing practitioner knowledge are separate responses.

    Read the supporting case summary ↑

  • Agreement ≠ Trust

    Agreement with a statement and its reported influence on trust answer different questions. Neither alone identifies what produces trust.

    Why distinguish them?

    Agreement can look like a favorable evaluation. Asking separately about reported influence on trust avoids treating that response as a trust mechanism.

    Read the supporting case summary ↑

  • Proposals ≠ Underlying needs

    Dispersed proposals can overlap. Synthesis supports collective decisions without establishing that a proposed solution was implemented.

    Why distinguish them?

    Counting proposals can make overlapping needs appear independent. Grouping them supports a shared decision structure; it does not validate or implement every idea.

    Read the supporting case summary ↑

  • Automation ≠ Judgment

    Restoring removed code and reviewing visual state distinction address different risks. Both Focus and Break retained labels.

    Why distinguish them?

    A successful automated test can be mistaken for complete accessibility assurance. Code restoration did not answer the separate perceptual question raised by planned human review.

    Read the supporting case summary ↑

  • Information ≠ Understanding

    Information organized by office may not answer residents’ practical questions. Clearer information does not establish a turnout effect.

    Why distinguish them?

    Publishing information can be mistaken for making it usable. Organizing around residents’ questions addresses an information structure while participation also depends on other conditions.

    Read the supporting case summary ↑

Usability ≠ retention and Compliance ≠ comprehension remain additional editorial questions. A telematics manuscript and supporting source review are pending; no causal connection is asserted here.

Practice

Find the gap. Trace the mechanism. Design what holds.

These are recurring moves in my work, not a fixed sequence. I return to the evidence when a decision or intervention reveals a new question.

My work connects organizational effectiveness, organizational development, systems strategy and human-centered design with organizational learning, responsible AI and organizational transformation. These are capabilities demonstrated through the work, rather than formal decision authority.

I swim upstream toward the problem.

orientation

When someone describes a problem as “like giving a fish legs,” my instinct is to swim with it.

Movement over resistance. Possibility before prescription.

  1. Find the gap

    Notice where a status, measure or explanation diverges from how the work actually happens.

  2. Trace the mechanism

    Investigate relationships among information, operating conditions and human behavior; test alternative explanations.

  3. Design what holds

    Equip leaders to decide, and help teams turn that understanding into practical change and shared knowledge.

What way of looking at this makes it appear true?

I examine both the structures producing behavior and the frames producing my explanations. These moves recur and overlap; new evidence can send me back to any of them.

  1. Notice — Identify a pattern, contradiction or unexpected outcome.
  2. Frame — Examine the assumptions and categories shaping the explanation.
  3. Model — Represent relationships that might explain the outcome.
  4. Stress test — Compare the explanation with evidence, exceptions and alternatives.
  5. Look for missing variables — Ask what the model excludes or obscures.
  6. Revise the model — Improve the explanation and examine what changes about action.
Inspect an illustrative model revision

Initial explanation: “The task is closed, so the work is complete.” Challenge: clarification continues after closure. Revised question: what did closure verify, and what remains unresolved?

This is a conceptual example, not a reconstruction of an operational record or proof of a causal mechanism. Alternative explanations could include changing requirements, missing information or an inappropriate completion criterion.

*About this experiment

A portfolio for experimentation, connected ideas, and the perspective of its creator, Sami Belina. The website itself is part of the inquiry.

The idea

A portfolio for experimentation, connecting ideas, and sharing its creator’s perspective. The website itself is part of that inquiry.

The working hypothesis

If a multidisciplinary approach is applied to website development, and the creator understands the language and methods being combined, can prompting a large language model produce new forms of web experience and communication?

The site tests the boundaries of what a website is expected to be and what it can become, while keeping its meaning accessible through reading, keyboard use and static alternatives. The question is whether that approach produces a meaningfully different experience from prompting for familiar page layouts. That remains an open question, not a demonstrated result.

The process

My writing, research, and observations inform the design; agents suggest structures and connections; I review their interpretation. We build small examples, inspect behavior, record sources and uncertainties, and revise.

The implementation

Built with React, TypeScript, and Vite for a Cloudflare Worker, with LLM-assisted development. This version includes reading templates, versioned content contracts, and explained connections. An isolated local writing trial has been exercised with synthetic manuscripts; hosted CMS integration and publication of new writing remain unimplemented. Original manuscripts stay separate from suggested edits. Automated checks provide technical evidence; human reading and accessibility review are part of the continuing work.

The interactive reading experiments ask whether making assumptions, evidence, and relationships inspectable helps visitors understand how I investigate systems. We record interface observations as the site evolves. Improved comprehension has not been established; technical checks are not a substitute for evaluating it with readers.

Reveal the hidden work

Does retaining a simple process while revealing the work beneath it help visitors inspect what the overview leaves out? This process is illustrative, not case evidence. Its lines encode relationships, not measured duration.

Technical observations: one reveal opens the extra steps in place; the original track remains visible; HTML and SVG contain the explanation without animation. Optional p5.js motion pauses offscreen and supports pause/reset and reduced motion. These are interface checks, not evidence of improved comprehension. Reader evaluation remains to be done.
