# Reader evaluation protocol — draft, 16 November 2026

This is a lightweight, moderated protocol for testing whether the site helps readers understand. No sessions have been run. This repository holds no visitor data, analytics or results. Do not add analytics or automatic collection for this study.

## Hypotheses (stated before testing)

- H1: After a short homepage visit, readers can say what kind of problems Sami investigates.
- H2: Readers can find an investigation (once one is published) and identify Sami’s individual contribution.
- H3: Readers can tell an observed outcome from an interpretation or a proposed change.
- H4: Readers can tell demonstrated facilitation experience from future educational interests.
- H5: Readers can explain why two linked subjects relate, and where the comparison breaks.
- H6: The Atlas map adds understanding beyond its text account. The alternative is that it adds confusion.

Each hypothesis can fail. Record alternative explanations for what you see, such as prior familiarity, task wording, device or time pressure.

## Participants and setup

- 5–8 voluntary adult readers per round: a mix of hiring or research evaluators and curious general readers.
- Use the reader’s own device where possible. Record browser, device, and any assistive technology they normally use.
- Consent covers notes only. Recording needs separate consent. Store notes privately, outside Git, without names.

## Tasks

| Task | Prompt | Observe | Success signal |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A Recognition | View the homepage for about 30 seconds, then: “What does this person investigate?” | Words used; what they scrolled to | Describes visible outcomes with unclear explanations, in their own words |
| B Evidence | “Find a piece of work and tell me what Sami personally did.” | Path; dead ends | Separates own contribution from shared work (needs a published case) |
| C Epistemic | Point to one passage: “Is this something observed, inferred or proposed?” | Reasoning | Correct category, with a reason |
| D Learning | “What experience does Sami have helping others understand? What is a future interest?” | Confusions | Keeps the two apart (needs `/learning/`) |
| E Exploration | “Follow a connection. Why are these related? Where does the comparison stop?” | Use of the explanation | Names a difference, not only a similarity |
| F Atlas | Half the readers start with the text account, half with the map; then swap. “What can you conclude from two cases being close?” | Over-inference | Says nearness alone does not establish a shared cause |

## Observation template

For each participant and task, record:

- Participant code
- Device and browser
- Assistive technology, if any
- Task
- Start and end time
- Path taken, as URLs
- What the reader said, near verbatim
- What the observer saw
- Errors or detours
- The observer’s interpretation, labelled as such
- Alternative explanations
- Severity: blocks, slows, or cosmetic

## Debrief questions

- What was easiest or hardest to find?
- Was anything confusing or surprising?
- Did any label promise something the page did not deliver?
- What would you need to trust a finding here?

## Reporting

Report what was observed, how many readers it applied to (n of N), and what remains uncertain. Distinguish findings from recommendations. Do not claim the site improved understanding unless a comparison supports it. Note the small, self-selected sample.
